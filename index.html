Which roofs in Padova should get solar panels, green roofs, or both?

Ahmadi, Codato, Peroni, De Marchi · University of Padova (DICEA) · My role: first author. Data processing, model design, analysis, maps and most of the writing.

Cities usually plan rooftops for one technology at a time. In this study I built a model, called RT4ES (Rooftops for Equity and Sustainability), that looks at every building in Padova and checks four options together: extensive green roofs, intensive green roofs, rooftop gardens and photovoltaics. The model shows where these options compete for the same roof, where they can be combined, and which neighbourhoods should come first.

How it works

  • Normalised surface model (DSM minus DTM) from 0.5 m LiDAR, cleaned with a 5×5 median filter to remove chimneys and other noise.
  • Slope, aspect and usable area for each roof, checked visually on the 20 cm orthophoto.
  • Separate rules for each intervention (slope, minimum area, building use). North-facing facets are excluded for PV on sloped roofs.
  • Suitable roofs are then prioritised for 40 urban units with a weighted linear combination: physical suitability (0.25), environmental vulnerability (0.35) and socio-economic vulnerability (0.40).

What we found

The model identifies 11.67 km² of net suitable roof area on 29,493 buildings. PV is possible on the largest share of roofs (11.93 km² on 30,372 roofs), followed by extensive green roofs (7.25 km²). Rooftop gardens (3.69 km²) and intensive green roofs (2.71 km²) depend more on building type.

Greening the suitable roofs would add about 10.94 km² of vegetated surface, around 21% of the city's current green-space stock. On the 9.10 km² of PV-suitable roofs, the estimated production is about 0.837 TWh per year, roughly three times the city's household electricity use.

Once social and environmental vulnerability are added, the priorities move towards compact, underserved neighbourhoods in the east, north-east and centre, where feasibility and need overlap. The whole model runs on open data and free software, so another city can reuse it.

Ongoing research · Urban heat and Nature-based Solutions

Where is Padova hottest, and who lives there?

Green roofs, trees and pocket parks can cool a city, but if nobody checks where they go, the benefits often end up in areas that are already better off. In this work I combine land surface temperature, imperviousness and vegetation deficit with a social vulnerability index, to find places where Nature-based Solutions are feasible, respond to heat risk and are fair.

The heat mapping is also part of a book chapter on urban climate justice in Padova (CRC Press, in press). Part of the method work was developed during a research stay at ITC, University of Twente.

Method design · Presented at FOSS4G-IT & OSMit 2026, Trento

Mapping the solar panels already installed in Padova's Industrial Zone

Before estimating how much more solar an industrial district can host, you need to know what is already on the roofs. With Daniele Codato I designed an open-source workflow to inventory existing rooftop PV in the Zona Industriale di Padova, separate occupied from free roof area, and test how far cheaper satellite images can replace aerial photos.

Three steps

  • Reference inventory: PV arrays delineated building by building on the 20 cm orthophoto, with a second reviewer. This becomes the training and validation data.
  • Object-based Random Forest: segmentation of orthophoto plus LiDAR height, with spectral, index, texture (GLCM) and geometry features to separate panels from dark roofing.
  • Cross-sensor test: the same classifier on 3 m PlanetScope SuperDove imagery, comparing 8 and 4 bands.

Why it is interesting

Skylights, dark membranes and HVAC units look like panels from above, so the workflow treats them as known false positives from the start. PlanetScope has no SWIR band, which makes the test a useful measure of how much PV signal is left in the visible and near-infrared range.

Status: method designed and presented; journal manuscript in preparation. Validation against the GSE Atlaimpianti register.

Remote sensing · MSc course project (2023)

Land cover of the Venice Lagoon from Sentinel-2

The lagoon is a place where sea-level rise, urban growth and vegetation change meet. I downloaded Sentinel-2 imagery, built a band set from the 10 m visible and near-infrared bands, clipped the study area and trained a supervised classification in QGIS with the Semi-Automatic Classification Plugin.

The first training set did not work well: the spectral signatures of rivers, roads and bare land overlapped. I rebuilt the training polygons, removed roads and beach (10 m pixels could not separate them from bare land) and split water into lagoon sea, canals and lakes.

Overall accuracy reached at least 95%, with 99% for water and 98% for vegetation. Bare land (90%) and buildings (85%) stayed lower, which matches the overlap I saw in the signatures.

Ecosystem services · MSc team project (2024)

Carbon storage and land-use change on an agroecological farm

Terre del Fiume is a sustainable farm in the south-west of Padova. With Noorullah Muradi I mapped its land use in 2016 and 2023 from georeferenced imagery, assigned carbon pools to each class and modelled carbon storage with InVEST, for the course of Agroecology and Territorial Management.

Stored carbon went from about 695 to 728 Mg C, a gain linked to tree planting, conservation and more diverse crops. Small losses appear where urban fabric expanded.
